1.1a
Looking at the return plot, Dyna-PPO reaches a high return level (~40) around
step 4000, while Plain PPO does not reach that same level until around step
11000-12000. So Dyna-PPO gets there roughly 7000-8000 steps sooner.

1.1b
Yes, a small one. At step 1000 both curves are almost identical (~20-22
return). At step 2000, Plain PPO (~28) is actually ahead of Dyna-PPO
(~23-24), this is the model learning penalty: The dynamics model is still
inaccurate this early (see 1.2a), so the imagined rollouts used for extra
updates are not helpful yet and slightly hold Dyna-PPO back. By step 3000
the two are about equal again, and from step 4000 onward Dyna-PPO pulls
clearly ahead. So the penalty is real but short-lived (roughly steps
1000-3000).

1.2a
At step 2000 the one-step MSE is still high (~5e-2, the peak of the curve),
and at this point PPO is actually ahead of Dyna-PPO (see 1.1b), the model
is too inaccurate yet to help. By step 4000, MSE has dropped to around
3e-2, and this is where Dyna-PPO pulls clearly ahead of PPO. So the model
seems to need to get below roughly 3e-2 MSE before it starts being useful
for imagined updates. By step 7000-8000, MSE is below ~1e-2 and the model
is quite accurate, but by then PPO has caught up again, so very low MSE
alone does not guarantee a return advantage.

1.2b
Early in training, E_k grows fast and compounds: By k=20 the error is
roughly 4 (with a wide uncertainty band), and it already grows noticeably
past k=10. Late in training, E_k stays small and almost flat across all
k=1...20 (under ~0.1). This means: Early on, a short imag_horizon (around
5, maybe up to 10) is safe, but going to k=15-20 early in training would
feed the policy very wrong imagined data. Later in training, once the model
is accurate, a longer imag_horizon is fine since errors barely compound.
This matches using imag_horizon=5 as a reasonable middle ground.
